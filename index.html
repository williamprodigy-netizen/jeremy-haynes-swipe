What matters

The whole class is a show-rate class, and he charges $5,000 for it

Every one of the five systems he teaches exists to move one number: the percentage of registered people who actually turn up. He opens with “we're going to help you improve your show rates” and closes 86 minutes later on a $5,000 program. Taught by someone selling the fix at five grand a seat.

Urgency video first, information videos second &mdash; the order is the finding

On a client's co-living funnel he put a single urgency video at the top of the confirmation page: 13 US cities had just legalised the model, first movers win. That one video moved the webinar show rate 19 points. He then added 19 information “breakout” videos answering sales-team objections — those added only 4 points. His conclusion: nobody cares about the information until you have given them a reason to care. His numbers, unaudited

He live-screenshares his Stripe dashboard as the trust proof

The 8m58s confirmation-page video is not a “lock it in your calendar” video. He opens Stripe on screen, scrolls it live so you can see it is not a screenshot, and shows a 0.37% lifetime dispute rate across 7,241 transactions, plus 3–4% annualised churn on $5K–$10K/mo offers. The proof is the operational metric a scammer cannot fake, not a testimonial.

&ldquo;What do the AI models say about you&rdquo; is one of his five systems

He treats ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Google's AI overview as a funnel step. His example: a client whose real price was $17,000–$26,000 had an old podcast review quoting “it might be 5K” ranking in AI answers. Prospects arrived on calls anchored at $5K, saw $17K, and the close rate collapsed. We ran an AI visibility audit and scored 22/100 — he is treating that same score as a show-rate and close-rate input, not a branding vanity metric.

The didn't-show-up survey email, one line long

Subject line: feedback. Body: curious why you didn't show up tonight. Appreciate your feedback trying to improve. The answers become next month's confirmation-page breakout videos. It is the cheapest feedback loop in the whole system and we do not run it.

No price on any page. No guarantee anywhere.

The replay page writes out the entire MIM pitch — 7 weeks, video library, community, certification — and never names a number. Price appears only on the live call, and inside the class as $5,000. There is no risk reversal of any kind, on any asset. He substitutes the Stripe dispute rate for a guarantee.

Buy and Call, side by side, at the same moment

The closing slide is two QR codes under the words Buy and Call. Self-serve checkout and book-a-call are offered simultaneously rather than sequentially, so a ready buyer never has to sit through a call to hand over $5,000.

He refuses payment plans out loud, as positioning

“We don't have in any way, shape or form payment plans. It's either you got the money or you don't. We don't want to sell to people we have to chase.” Paired with the on-slide line “Expensive betrays you”, the no-plan policy is sold as a filter rather than apologised for.

Two businesses, one domain, and the cheap one is the content engine

/breakthroughsession sells a $2,000 in-person session to founders doing $200K+/mo, filmed for YouTube. He says the plainly: “I am doing this at a very cost effective rate to get content for the channel, because these act as a great top of funnel viewership generation source.” The offer is priced to be filmed, not to be profitable.

The build is disposable on purpose

bolt.new SPA, four Wistia videos, a Meta pixel and a Zapier catch hook. No ClickFunnels, no GHL, no CRM form. Total build is one page router and a webhook — which is why he could stand it up, run one 38-day flight, and abandon it without cost.
